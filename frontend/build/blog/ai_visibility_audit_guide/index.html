How to Audit Your Brand’s Presence in ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews

An AI audit helps you understand one thing. When people ask questions about your type of business in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, or Google AI Overviews, does your brand appear in the answers? These AI tools collect information from your website, business profiles, forums, and other online pages to prepare an answer. Unlike Google search, they do not show ten links and ask people to choose one. Instead, they provide an answer. This shift is also changing how B2B buyers research and compare companies, as explained in why zero-click search is reshaping B2B buyer journeys. So, when doing an audit, check a few things in order. First, find the questions your customers usually ask. Then, check which brands appear in the AI answers for each question. Next, review your score. Find out what content your competitors have published and what you are missing. Finally, fix the gaps one by one. You can do all this yourself using a spreadsheet. Why AI Search Visibility Matters More Than Traditional SEO Rankings

In traditional search, the main focus was on how high a website appeared in Google results. But in AI search, someone asks a question and gets a written answer. Sometimes, a few links also appear alongside it. What matters is whether your brand appears in that answer. Suppose one of your pages ranks third on Google. Even then, that page may not appear in an AI answer. This is because AI may choose content that gives the clearest answer. Ranking on Google and appearing in an AI answer are different. This is important for local businesses in Singapore. When someone asks an AI tool which company they should choose for a particular service, the answer may mention your competitor instead of your brand. In that case, you could miss out on a potential customer. Our AEO App tracks how your brand performs across four AI engines: ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews. Step 1: Find the Questions Your Customers Ask Across All AI Engines

Instead of starting with keywords, begin by finding the questions your customers usually ask. People often type short phrases into Google. But when using AI tools, they usually ask complete questions. For example, "how to improve ChatGPT visibility for my website" or "get my business cited in AI answer engines". To find these questions, check your customer emails, support tickets, and social media messages. Add every question you find to a spreadsheet. Then, search for those questions in ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews. Take a screenshot of each answer and save it. This will help you compare the results later. In the AEO App, this feature is the Prompts page. It shows questions people are likely to ask AI tools about a business like yours. Along with each question, you can see its intent (informational, commercial, or transactional) and estimated monthly searches. You can also add your own questions. We'll check these prompts in the next analysis. Once your prompt list is ready, select the questions that matter most to your business. These questions will be the starting point for creating content later. Prioritize questions your actual customers are likely to ask.

Step 2: Check Your Current Visibility Score and Citation Rate

Now, check each question in every AI engine. Pay attention to the following things in each answer:

  • Does your brand name appear?
  • Does the answer link to your website or business profile?
  • Does your brand name appear first, in the middle, or at the end of the answer?
  • Which competitors are mentioned instead of your brand? You can also score yourself.
  • If your brand appears with a link, give it 100 points.
  • If your brand name appears without a link, give it 50 points.
  • If it is not mentioned at all, give it 0 points. Calculate the average of the points across all questions. This is a simple score, but it helps you understand whether your visibility has improved when you repeat the audit later. Step 3: Identify Content Gaps and Competitor Positioning

Find the questions where a competitor appears in the AI answer, but your brand does not. Then open that competitor's website and read the page the AI may have used to gather the information. To understand the factors behind these recommendations, read how ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini choose what to recommend. Pay attention to how they write their content:

  • Is it a how-to guide?
  • Is it a comparison article?
  • Is it a list?
  • Is it a case study?
  • Does it answer the question in the first few lines?
  • Does it use clear headings and short paragraphs?
  • Does it have schema markup?
  • Is its Google Business Profile complete?
  • Does it have links from industry forums? This will help you find out what is missing from your own website. For example, a competitor may have published a "Top 10" list about your service in Singapore. But your website does not have similar content. This is a content gap. Similarly, if you do not have separate pages explaining the services you provide to small businesses, that is another area to work on. Our AEO App has a Competitors page for this. Step 4: Check Your Website's Technical and Content Readiness

AI engines need pages that are easy to read and trustworthy. So, check the following things on your website:

  • Page titles and meta descriptions: Are they clear and specific, or are they too general?
  • Headings: Have you used H1, H2, and H3 to divide the content into smaller sections?
  • Content length: Does the page have enough information to explain the topic clearly?
  • Schema markup: Have you added structured data such as business, product, or review schema?
  • Google Business Profile: Are all details accurate and up to date?
  • Internal links: Do related pages link to each other?
  • Backlinks: Are other websites related to your industry linking to your website?
  • Mobile experience: Does your website open quickly and remain easy to read on mobile phones? AI engines and Google crawl the same web. So, basic SEO practices are still important. However, remember one thing. Even if your website is well-optimized, AI may not choose your content if it does not answer the questions customers ask. Similarly, even if you have good content, AI may struggle to use it if your website is slow or lacks the right structure. Step 5: Prepare Your Audit Report and Create an Action Roadmap

Once the audit is complete, prepare a report with all your findings. Your report should include the following information:

  • Your current AI visibility score
  • The questions you tracked
  • The answers given by each AI engine and the sources they mentioned
  • Competitors that are performing better than you in those answers
  • Content gaps, such as missing guides, use-case pages, and content formats
  • Technical issues that need to be fixed on your website
  • Three to five content projects you have decided to work on first Choose the projects that are likely to bring the most benefit. Prioritize questions with higher search volume and clear buying intent. Also, if a competitor's brand appears for a question but your brand does not have relevant content, focus on creating that content.
