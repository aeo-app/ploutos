ChatGPT vs Claude Brand Citations: How Often Do They Match?

How Often Do ChatGPT and Claude Cite the Same Brands? A Comparative Analysis

Search has changed. Earlier, when we wanted to find something, we searched on Google and opened different websites. Now, we can ask AI tools and get answers directly.

People ask ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, or Gemini questions and get answers that mention a few brands. The brands mentioned often become the first options people consider.

This makes Brand AI Visibility important for marketing teams. If ChatGPT recommends your brand, will Claude recommend it too? This article explains why their answers differ and what this means for your AEO Strategy and Generative Engine Optimization work.

Do ChatGPT and Claude Name the Same Brands?

Sometimes, but not always. Both tools may mention well-known brands in an industry, but their recommendations can differ, especially for less familiar companies.

Be careful when someone quotes a figure such as “73% overlap” without explaining how it was calculated. AI answers can change depending on the question, timing, and settings. Without a clear method, it is hard to know whether that figure is reliable.

The main point is simple: one AI citing your brand doesn't mean all of them will.

Mention vs Citation: Two Different Things

A mention is when an AI includes your brand name in its answer. A citation is when it provides a source, usually a link, to support its answer.

For example, an AI might mention your company but link to a review site instead of your website.

One tool may mention your brand frequently but link to other websites, while another may link directly to your site. ChatGPT Citation Tracking should cover both mentions and citations to give you a clearer picture of your visibility.

Why Do ChatGPT and Claude Pick Different Brands?

Imagine asking two friends to recommend a restaurant. One might suggest a place they visit regularly, while the other recommends somewhere different. Their experiences and the reviews they trust may differ.

AI tools work similarly. They may use different information and sources, leading to different recommendations.

Different Training, Different Live Sources

Each AI model has been trained on different material, so the brands familiar to one model may differ from those familiar to another.

When an AI tool searches the internet, it may find different websites. Even the same tool may give you a different list when you ask the same question again.

Wording Changes the Answer

The way you ask a question can affect the answer.

For example, “best CRM for small teams” and “affordable CRM for a startup” may sound similar, but an AI tool might recommend different brands. The first question focuses on overall software quality, while the second focuses on affordability.

Your Website Is Not the Only Source

AI tools can use information from review websites, comparison articles, industry lists, online forums, and news coverage, not just your website.

Your website might explain your services clearly, but other websites may contain little information about your company. This could make it harder for an AI tool to find enough information to recommend your brand confidently.

No publicly available formula explains exactly how much importance each AI tool gives to each source. However, your website content and the information available about your brand elsewhere can both influence your AI visibility.

How the Type of Question Changes the Overlap

The overlap between ChatGPT and Claude isn't fixed. It depends on the type of question people ask.

  • Information Questions: Consider the question, “What is a CRM?” The AI tools may explain what CRM means without mentioning any brands. In that case, there's little to compare.
  • Recommendation Questions: Now consider, “What is the best project management tool for a small team?” Different AI tools may recommend well-known brands. One answer may include less familiar companies, while another may leave them out.
  • Comparison and Alternative Questions: People may ask questions such as “Tool A vs Tool B” or “What are the alternatives to Tool A?” Comparison articles and online reviews may influence AI answers. These questions matter because people asking them may be close to making a purchase. If your brand isn't mentioned, you could miss an opportunity to reach potential customers.

What Does This Look Like Across Industries?

The same issue can appear across industries, though the impact may vary.

  • SaaS: A buyer looking for a project management tool for a remote team might see your product recommended by ChatGPT but not Claude. Your brand may therefore be visible in one AI tool but not the other.
  • E-commerce: Someone looking for running shoes suitable for flat feet might receive different brand recommendations from different AI tools.
  • FinTech: Trust is especially important in financial technology. Someone looking for an app to send money abroad may receive different recommendations depending on the AI tool. Clear, consistent information about a company helps AI tools understand what it does.
  • B2B Services: A manager looking for agencies that provide cybersecurity audits might receive a list of three or four companies. Businesses missing from that answer may lose an opportunity to reach a potential customer.

Why Does Manual Tracking Become Difficult?

Many teams start by asking ChatGPT and Claude a few questions, recording the answers in a spreadsheet, and repeating the process every week.

This is an easy way to start without paying for a tool. However, it becomes difficult to manage as the number of questions grows.

A Small Sample and Constantly Changing Answers

Manual checking may cover twenty or thirty questions, but customers can ask thousands of different questions in their own words.

AI answers can also change from day to day. A result recorded on Monday may be different on Friday, making earlier records less reliable.

Unfair Comparisons

Whether an AI tool searches the internet can affect its answer.

For example, comparing a ChatGPT answer that uses live web search with a Claude answer that does not may be unfair. The two tools gather information differently, so their results may not be directly comparable.

Knowing the Result Is Not Enough

Manual checking tells you whether your brand appeared in an answer, but it doesn't always explain why.

Your website might have gaps in its schema markup, AI tools might struggle to find your pages, or your competitors might have stronger content.

Check which competitors appear alongside your brand and which brands are recommended when yours is missing. Otherwise, you could spend time making changes that don't solve the actual problem.
