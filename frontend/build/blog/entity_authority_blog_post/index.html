Entity Authority: How LLMs Decide Which Brands to Trust

Building Entity Authority: How LLMs Decide Which Brands to Trust

Think about the last time you asked a friend, "Where should we eat tonight?"

Your friend didn't give you ten options. They said, "Go to this place. Trust me." And you went.

That is exactly how people use ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and Gemini now. They ask a question, and the AI answers like a trusted friend, with two or three names and a reason for each. Most people go with what they're told.

So here is the real question for your business: when someone asks AI about your category, does it say your name?

That is what Brand AI Visibility is about, and it depends on one thing: how much the AI trusts you. In this post, I'll explain how that trust is built.

What "Entity Authority" Means

An "entity" is simply a clear, recognisable thing: a brand, a product, a person, or a company.

Ask ten people for a good mechanic, and they'll name the shop everyone knows, that other mechanics respect, and that's been around for years. Nobody picks a garage they have never heard of.

AI tools work similarly. They try to understand who you are, what you do, and whether other people vouch for you. If the picture is clear and consistent, they feel safe recommending you. If it's patchy or contradictory, they skip you.

Entity authority is that clear, trusted picture. It's the core of any serious AEO Strategy and a big part of Generative Engine Optimization.

How AI Decides Which Brands to Trust

No one outside the AI companies knows the exact formula, and anyone who claims to is guessing. But some signals clearly matter, and they match common sense.

  1. Your story is the same everywhere. Your name, what you sell, and who you serve should read the same on your website, your LinkedIn page, your app store listing, and your directory profiles. If one place calls you a "marketing tool" and another calls you a "design agency," the AI gets confused.
  2. Other people say good things about you. What you say about yourself counts for less than what others say. Reviews, news mentions, partner pages, and fair comparison articles all act like references on a job application.
  3. Your website proves its claims. Saying "we're the best" proves nothing. Case studies, pricing details, honest comparisons, and clear answers to real questions give the AI something solid to rely on.
  4. Your pages are easy for machines to read. Behind every page is code. Labels in that code tell a machine "this is a product," "this is a price," or "this is a question and its answer." Without those labels, the AI has to guess.
  5. You stay focused. A brand known for one thing is easier to recommend than one that claims to do everything.
  6. Your information is current. Old prices, dead pages, and outdated details make you look unreliable.

Why Shortcuts Don't Build Trust

Some people look for a quick trick to get AI to mention them. They buy lots of reviews, or put up many pages that say nothing useful. This usually does not work.

Why? AI doesn't listen only to you. It also looks at what your customers and other websites say about you. If your words and their words don't match, the AI has no reason to believe you.

Think of a tea shop with a big board that says "Best Tea in Town." The board proves nothing. But when people keep telling each other, "Go there, the tea is really good," that is real trust.

It works the same way here. Real customers, honest pages, and fair mentions add up slowly. There is no overnight trick, and anyone who promises one is not being honest.

Why Doing It Yourself Gets Messy

Once you know the signals, it's tempting to handle it all in-house. Here is why that usually stalls:

  • You can't easily see the results. Ask ChatGPT the same question twice, and the answer can change. Add Claude, Perplexity, and Gemini, and one person can't realistically keep up.
  • It is time consuming. Checking answers, comparing against competitors, and fixing pages is a weekly job, not a one-time task.
  • Things keep changing. AI tools update often, so what worked last month may not work now.
  • Mistakes hide. A missing label or a broken page can quietly block you for months before anyone notices.

That is a lot of effort for something you can't clearly measure.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is entity authority?

Entity authority is how clearly an AI understands your brand and how much it trusts you. A brand described consistently across many reliable sources is easier for an AI to recommend.

How do AI engines choose which brands to mention?

Companies don't publish their exact methods. In practice, AI answers tend to favour brands that are clearly described, consistently presented, backed by trusted outside sources, and supported by useful content on their own sites.

Do reviews and mentions on other websites really matter?

Yes. What others say about you is stronger proof than what you say about yourself. Reviews, news mentions, partner pages, and fair comparison articles show that real people vouch for you.

Can a small or new brand get cited?

Yes. Smaller brands can be cited when they are focused, clear about what they do, and supported by real proof. Size matters less than clarity and trust.

How long does it take to see results?

It varies by industry, competition, and starting point, and no honest provider can promise a fixed timeline. Tracking your mentions regularly is the best way to see whether your changes are working.
