How to Correct Wrong Brand Information in AI Answers

How to Correct Wrong Brand Information in AI Answers

Open ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, or Claude and ask about your company as a customer would. Ask what you sell, how much it costs, who it is for, and how you compare with others.

Now read the answers properly. Is everything in the way you expected?

If yes, great. If something is wrong, you are not alone. Many brands have never checked this, and people already use these tools to decide what to buy. That’s why Brand AI Visibility matters now. It’s not just about being mentioned; it’s about being mentioned correctly.

What Kind of Wrong Information Shows Up

Wrong answers don’t all look the same. These are the ones brands see most:

  • Old details. Prices, plans, features, or offers that you changed long ago.
  • Missing details. The AI skips a product or service you actually offer.
  • Mixed-up details. Your facts get mixed up with another company with a similar name.
  • Wrong category. It describes you as something you are not.
  • Wrong comparisons. The AI says a competitor does something you don’t, or vice versa.
  • Made-up details. Sometimes the AI adds something that was never true.

It helps to note which type you are facing, because the fix differs for each one.

Why AI Gets It Wrong

AI doesn’t know your company the way your team does. It pulls information from different places and stitches it into one answer. When that information is outdated, unclear, or inconsistent across sources, the answer comes out wrong.

Usually it’s one of these: an old page is still online, your own website doesn’t say something clearly, other websites say something different about you, or there is no strong page that proves the fact. So it’s rarely a random mistake. AI works with what it finds.

What a Wrong Answer Costs You

Think about how people use AI today. They ask one question and read one short answer. They don’t open websites like they used to.

So if the answer is wrong, customers make decisions based on bad information. This matters in every field, whether you run a SaaS product, an online store, a FinTech service, or a B2B company. And you won’t know about most of these lost customers, because nobody writes to tell you why they left.

What Actually Fixes a Wrong Answer

There is no edit button. Some tools have a thumbs-down or feedback option, and you can use it, but it doesn’t promise any change. The answer changes only when the information behind it changes. In practice, a proper correction has these parts:

  1. Find where the wrong detail comes from. It could be your own site, an old listing, a review site, or a news article. Until you know the source, you are fixing blindly.
  2. Make your own pages say it clearly. If the AI is wrong about your pricing, there should be one clear, up-to-date pricing page. If it is wrong about who you serve, your pages should say that in plain words. AI picks up clear statements much more easily than hints.
  3. Give proof. Case studies, policy pages, comparison pages, and pages with real details give the AI something solid to trust. A claim with no proof page is easy to ignore.
  4. Add structured data. Schema labels your facts, like name, price, address, and product type, so machines can read them without guessing.
  5. Clean up the other places. Old directory listings, outdated articles, and partner pages may still carry the old version. If they disagree with your site, the AI may believe them instead.
  6. Answer the real questions people ask. FAQ sections written around real customer questions give the AI ready, accurate answers to choose from.
  7. Check again. After the changes, you need to see whether the answers actually moved in each engine, not just once.

Look at that list again. It’s not a one-hour job. It’s research, writing, technical work, and follow-up, repeated whenever something changes.

Why Doing It Manually Is Hard

Doing all this by hand has three big problems. First, you see only a small part. The same question can return four different answers across four engines, and different answers on different days, so a few manual checks tell you little about what real customers see. Second, it takes a lot of time, and most teams do it once and then forget it. Third, things keep changing: engines change how they pick sources, and competitors keep publishing, so a fix that worked last month may not hold.

That is why a good AEO Strategy needs steady tracking, not just occasional checks.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I ask ChatGPT or another AI to correct wrong information about my brand?

Not in a reliable way. You can use the feedback option where it exists, but it doesn’t guarantee a change. Answers improve when the pages and data the AI relies on are clearer and consistent.

Why does AI still show old details after I updated my website?

Your website is only one source. Old pages, third-party listings, and review sites may still have the earlier information, and AI engines may use those. Until the sources agree, you may keep getting mixed answers.

How do I find where a wrong answer came from?

Check which sources each AI engine cites or uses for that question. Doing this by hand for many questions is slow. Citation tracking makes it easier to find the page behind the mistake.

How long does it take for a correction to show up?

There is no fixed time. It depends on the engine, how it collects information, and how clear your sources are. Keep checking the answers after every fix instead of assuming it worked.

Is fixing wrong AI answers the same as normal SEO?

No. SEO is mostly about getting your page to rank in a list of results. Answer Engine Optimization is about getting your brand understood and quoted correctly inside the AI’s answer. A page can rank well and still be quoted wrongly.
