ChatGPT vs Perplexity vs Gemini: Which Drives More Traffic?

ChatGPT vs. Perplexity vs. Gemini: Which AI Search Engine Drives More Brand Traffic?

Imagine a customer walking into a shop and asking, "Which laptop is best for my budget?" The shopkeeper names two or three brands, and the customer usually buys from one of them.

People now ask AI tools like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini the same kind of question. These tools give one answer with just a few brand names. If your brand is not one of them, that customer may never hear about you.

This is why Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) is getting so much attention. In this article, we'll look at how the three tools differ, and why it is hard for a normal business to know which one is sending customers.

So, Which One Sends More Traffic?

There is no single honest answer, so be careful with anyone who gives you one. The result changes with your industry, your customers' questions, and even the day you check. A software company may be mentioned often in one tool and almost never in another. An online shop may see the opposite.

What we can say is how each tool generally behaves:

  • ChatGPT: Millions of people use it daily for everyday questions. When it searches the web, it writes an answer and can show the sources it used.
  • Perplexity: It works like a search engine that answers in plain language. It usually lists the websites behind its answer, so readers can open them easily.
  • Gemini: It is Google's AI. It is closely connected to Google Search, so it matters most for brands that already depend on Google to reach customers.

So the better question is not "which tool is best?" but "where is my brand mentioned, and where is it missing?" That is what Brand AI Visibility means.

Why Getting Mentioned Is Not the Same as Ranking

In a normal Google search, you try to reach page one. In AI search, there is no page one. The tool writes a single answer and includes a few brands. You are either in it, or you are not, and there is no "position 7" to fall back on. A customer who gets one clear answer rarely scrolls to look for more.

Two tools can also answer the same question differently. One recommends you, another recommends your competitor, and a third does not know you exist. That is why Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is not just SEO with a new name. The goal is different, and so is the way you measure it.

Mentions and Citations: Two Different Wins

When an AI tool answers a question, your brand can show up in two ways:

  • A mention is when the AI names your brand in its answer.
  • A citation is when it lists your website as a source, so the customer can click through.

They do not always come together. A brand can be named without a link, and a website can be cited while the answer recommends someone else. The best case is to have both.

That is also why website visits alone don't tell the full story. A customer may read your name in an answer and visit later by searching for you, so the visit looks like normal organic traffic. Tracking both mentions and citations gives the real picture.

Why Doing This Yourself Is Harder Than It Looks

Many teams start by asking the three tools a few questions and noting which brands appear. It feels easy, but it breaks down quickly.

  • The answers keep changing. The same question can get a different answer on a different day or with a slightly different wording. One check tells you very little. You need many questions, asked many times.
  • Every tool is different. Your brand may show up in ChatGPT, be missing in Gemini, and appear only as a side note in Perplexity. Each one needs separate tracking, and all of them keep updating.
  • Finding the reason is harder than finding the problem. It's easy to see that a competitor is mentioned and you are not. Knowing why is not. It could be unclear pages, missing structured data, a site that AI tools can't read well, or no proof pages. Fixing it then means rewriting pages and building new ones.

A manual check shows a small part of the picture. It cannot give you the full view or tell you what to do next.

What Usually Decides If an AI Mentions Your Brand

The companies behind these tools don't share every detail, so no one outside can say exactly how they choose sources. In general, AI tools favour sources that are clear, trustworthy, and easy to read. These things usually help:

  • Pages that answer real customer questions directly. If a customer asks, "Is this good for small shops?", a page that answers in plain words is easier for an AI tool to use.
  • Proof. Comparison pages, case studies, and clear product details give the tool something solid to rely on.
  • A website AI tools can read without trouble. If a page is hard to read or lacks structured data, the tool may skip it.
  • Brand information that stays the same across the web. If your name, product details, or pricing differ from place to place, the tool has less reason to trust any of them.

If these are missing, an AI tool may skip your brand even when your product is good. Most businesses don't know which ones they are missing, and that is the gap a proper platform is built to close.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which AI search engine sends the most traffic to websites?

It depends on your industry, your audience, and the questions people ask. Track ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini together instead of guessing.

Why does my brand show up in one AI tool but not in the others?

Each tool works differently, and companies don't share every detail. So the same question can get different answers in different tools.

Do I need to optimize separately for each AI tool?

The basics are the same for all of them: clear pages, proof pages, a readable website, and consistent brand information. But your results can differ from tool to tool, so each one needs its own tracking.

What is Share of Model?

Share of Model shows how often your brand appears in AI answers compared with your competitors. It tells you how much of the AI conversation in your industry your brand owns.
