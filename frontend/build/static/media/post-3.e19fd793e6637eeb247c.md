Traditional keyword research asks "what words does my buyer search for?" That question made sense when search boxes rewarded short, high-volume phrases. It's the wrong question for an answer engine.

## The actual question

The right question is: what full sentence does someone type into ChatGPT or Perplexity when they're two weeks from a buying decision?

That sentence is longer, more specific, and usually contains a comparison or a constraint — "best project management tool for a 10-person agency that already uses Slack," not "project management software."

## How to cluster around it

1. **Collect real prompts, not keywords.** Pull actual questions from support tickets, sales call transcripts, and community forums — anywhere a real buyer phrased their problem in their own words.
2. **Group by constraint, not by product category.** "For a small team," "on a tight budget," "that integrates with X" are the clusters that matter, not "project management tools" as a flat category.
3. **Write one page per constraint cluster**, answering that specific framing directly in the first two paragraphs, rather than one page trying to cover every angle.

## Why this beats broad-match keyword pages

A page trying to rank for "project management software" in the old sense has to stay generic enough to match dozens of intents. A page written for "10-person agency already on Slack" can be maximally specific — and specificity is exactly what gets quoted back to someone who typed a specific question.
